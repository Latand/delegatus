### How to open them

The prototypes are the real Viewer over the kanban evidence fixture (`src/components/kanban/issue1695Evidence.fixture.tsx`, scenarios `stages`, `issue1820` for the Overview, `issue1820-empty` for an empty Overview, and `stages&files-pending=1` for a project whose board is still loading) with the variant's chrome around it. The existing kanban browser driver renders all of them:

```
LLV_KANBAN_BROWSER_TEST=1 CHROME_BIN=<chromium> \
  bun test src/components/kanban/kanbanBoard.browser.test.tsx -t "interface redesign"
```

Run it with `HOME`, `TMPDIR`, `LLV_STATE_DIR` under the OS temp root and `LLV_VIEWER_CONTROL_URL` on a closed port, as every driver run from a lane is. The block has two tests. The first draws the frames into `LLV_INTERFACE_REDESIGN_OUT` (default `.artifacts/interface-redesign/`, never committed) as `v<number>-<width>-<language>[-dark]-<state>.png` and writes `evidence/interface-redesign/measurements.json`: 451 frames, every state at 1440×900, 1000×800 and 390×844, in English and Ukrainian, and every variant but 0 once more in dark. The second walks every new surface from the keyboard and every phone sheet through Back, its handle, × and the scrim, and writes `evidence/interface-redesign/interaction.json`. `LLV_INTERFACE_REDESIGN_ONLY=1,7`, `LLV_INTERFACE_REDESIGN_FRAMES=390` and `LLV_INTERFACE_REDESIGN_LANGS=uk` narrow a run.

The prototypes are interactive in that frame: the palette filters, the rail collapses, menus drill in and return, toggles and segments switch, Settings changes section, a phone row that leads to another menu changes the sheet. A row that would start a product action (create a project, stop a host) does nothing: the design-only contract keeps product actions inert.

Content the prototype draws itself (five projects, their counts, limit percentages, 38 agents) is invented. The board, the orchestrator pane and the phone screens under it are the fixture's own synthetic content. No frame of the live installation is in this repository or this pull request; the private reference frames from the audit stayed where the audit left them.

### How a variant is attached to the real Viewer

Five mechanisms, all in `src/components/kanban/interfaceRedesign.prototype.tsx`:

1. **Navigation as a layout sibling.** A variant with its own navigation hides the real rail with one style rule and draws its rail beside the Viewer in the same flex row. Nothing floats. Variants 1 and 7 place their two controls inside whichever header names the place: the project board's bar, the bar of a loading or empty project (`DashboardBar`), or the Overview's title row.
2. **Existing buttons answer.** Regrouped menus open from the buttons that exist today: the rail's "⋯", the board's "⋯", a card's "⋯", the phone's "⋯" on the board, a task and a conversation, and the phone's project title. No trigger is added for a menu. The phone's "⋯" opens the menu of the screen it is on, and tells the Overview from a project's board. The surface that opens is a dialog, so each intercepted button declares `aria-haspopup="dialog"` where the product declares a menu, says `aria-expanded` true while it is open and false after, and gets back what it declared when the prototype is taken away.
3. **The product's own surface contracts.** A desktop menu, palette or dialog is a `useModalLayer` layer (`src/components/modalLayer.ts`): a scrim takes presses outside it, Tab and Shift+Tab stay inside it, Escape closes it, focus lands on its field or first control and returns to the control that opened it, and a drill-in moves focus to its Back row and back. A phone surface is the product's `MobileSheet` (`src/components/mobile/MobileSheet.tsx`), opened and closed through `mobileNav.openSheet` and `closeSheet` under the sheet name the product already uses, so it adds one history entry, Back closes it before the screen, its handle drags it shut past 80 px, and × and the scrim close it.
4. **One stylesheet for glass.** Variant 6 changes fills, edges, radii, shadows and the stacking of the board header, and changes no box.
5. **A row of the layout for the attention notice.** In 1, 2, 3 and 7 the header is shorter than today's at 1000 px, and the notice, which the Viewer hangs from its badge at a fixed distance from the top of the window, ended over the orchestrator pane's controls. Those variants give the notice a band under the header (above the board's body, after any filter row): 44 px, present only while a notice is shown, with the notice in it as one line, its question and then the conversation's title. The band is measured from the layout, so the header may be one row or two, and nothing but the band is under the notice. The price is that the board moves down 44 px while a notice is shown and back when it is dismissed. Variants 4, 5 and 6 keep today's header and today's notice.

The rows of every regrouped menu and the home of every function come from `interfaceRedesign.prototype.model.ts`. An inventory entry that is a whole menu (W3, W4, W8) or holds two controls (B3, B11) is spelled out action by action, and `interfaceRedesign.prototype.model.test.ts` fails when any one action has no row, and proves it by taking each action out in turn.

- glass trapped the attention notice under the orchestrator pane in 6 and 7 (0 of 15 points answered at 1440);
- the phone "⋯" in 4, 5 and 7 opened the conversation menu on a task and on a pipeline;
- the new palette, menus and Settings had no focus management, and / did nothing in 3;
- the new phone surfaces bypassed `MobileSheet` and `mobileNav`, so Back, the handle and history did not behave;
- 1 and 7 had no way into navigation or System on the Overview;
- direction B's card menu had no place for the hold reason, and the model test did not notice;
- the crown control was drawn at 1.5:1 to 1.7:1, and 6 kept one text at 2.95:1.

A second read-only review found four more, each in a place the driver had excused or had no state for:

- at 1000 px in 1, 2, 3 and 7 the product's notice covered 8 to 16 of the 28 px of the orchestrator pane's "Show reports" and "Dock at the side", and the corners of "Collapse" and the resize grip. - in 4, 5 and 7 the intercepted "⋯" buttons kept the product's `aria-haspopup="menu"` while opening a dialog. They declare a dialog now, and the keyboard walk compares what each entry declares with the role of what it opens.

## Visual language: variant 6

The glass treatment follows one rule: the navigation layer is glass, and what is read stays opaque. Rail, bars, the phone's top bar and dock, menus, popovers and sheets become translucent and blur what is behind them; cards, the conversation feed and the message field keep their solid surface. Columns are translucent without a blur, because they are large and they scroll. A soft three-colour wash (the emblem's warm tone, the accent's indigo, a faint green) sits under everything so the translucency has something to show. Radii grow one step (controls 8 to 10 px, surfaces 12 to 16 px). Motion is the product's own 200 ms ease on menus, and none under reduced motion. With `prefers-reduced-transparency` the fills return to today's solid surfaces and every blur, the menus' and sheets' included, is off.

This is the reading of Apple's guidance used here: glass belongs to controls and navigation that float above content, content itself stays legible on a solid ground, and blur marks a layer, never decoration (`ui-ux-pro-max`: `blur-purpose`, `color-accessible-pairs`, `reduced-motion`; `frontend-design`: no glass on everything, no glow, tinted neutrals). Apple's guideline pages need a browser session this lane did not use for them, so no rule here is quoted from Apple.

### Findings a build must carry

- **A bar that hosts a fixed notice must not become an isolated stacking context below its neighbours.** A `backdrop-filter` on the board header makes it the containing block of the attention notice it hosts, so the blur is drawn by a layer under the header's content. The first draft isolated the header for that layer, and the notice, though still at its coordinates, went under the orchestrator pane: 0 of 15 points answered at 1440, 5 of 15 at 1000. The project header's stacking context now sits at the island's own layer (50), and the Overview's bar, whose island is outside it, is only isolated; on layer 50 it covered the notice in its turn. The driver now hit-tests the notice and every real control against today's frame, which is how both were found.
- **Secondary labels need one step more ink on glass.** Over a dimmed backdrop the muted label colour read 4.2:1 on a menu. On glass surfaces the prototype mixes 30 % of the primary text colour into the muted one, and menus and sheets use an 86 % fill. Both belong in `tokens.css` with a case in `tokens.contrast.test.ts`.
- **The engine name in the rail's limits footer is under AA today** (3.03:1, the engine's tint on the card) and read 2.95:1 on glass. Variant 6 draws it in the text ink; the gate now has no exemption for inherited defects, and a build that keeps the footer should give that label an AA ink everywhere.
- **Functional icons need 3:1 too.** The crown at rest was drawn in the border colour (1.5:1 to 1.7:1), and pinned in the crown gold (2.2:1 on a light card). It is now the muted ink at rest and, pinned, a gold fill inside a warning-ink outline; switch tracks are muted ink off and accent on; a chosen segment has an accent edge.
- **A shorter header needs a row for the notice.** The Viewer hangs the notice from its badge, fixed 56 px from the top of the window, and today's two-row header at 1000 px (88 px) is tall enough for most of its 62 px. Wherever a variant's header is one row (1, 2, 3 and 7 at 1000 px: 48 px) the pane under it starts higher and the notice lay over its controls: 16 of the 28 px of "Show reports" and "Dock at the side" in 2 and 3, 8 px in 1 and 7, and the corners of "Collapse the orchestrator chat" and the resize grip. The prototype gives the notice a row of the layout under the header (mechanism 5), and the driver holds it: the notice may cover no control today's frame reaches and may cut no control or text it does not cut today. A build renders the notice in that row from `Viewer.tsx`, in place of the fixed anchor, and gives `AttentionToast` the one-line face; the Overview's bar keeps today's 236 px reserve for the badge alone. Collapsing the notice into the badge after a few seconds, as the phone's banner does, would shorten the time the board is moved, and is a separate decision.
- **Blur costs frames on a slow machine, and the cost needs a gate on real devices.** Two reviews measured it in Chromium with a software GPU and the CPU throttled four times. The first: a desktop p95 frame time of 233 to 500 ms for 6 and 167 to 233 ms for 7, against 183 and 133 ms with the blur off. The second, over 27 probes with 300 more tasks on the board (243 cards in the DOM): variant 6 at 1000 px, p95 33.4 ms with glass against 16.8 ms with the blur off, twice the frame time; phone emulation stayed at 16.7 to 16.8 ms. Today's large board already janks without glass. No physical phone or desktop GPU was measured. Variants 6 and 7 therefore carry a gate before any glass ships: the frame times on the operator's own desktop and phone, with the blur on and off, on a board of the live size; and blur stays off the large rail first.
- **In dark the wash is faint.** Dark glass reads mostly as today's dark with softer edges. That is honest to the restraint the requirement asks for; a stronger dark wash would cost contrast on the columns.

Cost: about 45 style rules and seven tokens (`src/styles/tokens.css`, `kanbanBoard.css`, the phone bar and sheet classes), the stacking rule for the two headers, an AA ink for the engine label, and the contrast cases; two lanes, and before them the device gate above, which is a measurement and no code. Risk: low for layout (nothing moves, measured, and the notice is held by the driver), medium for stacking (a new stacking context on any surface that hosts a fixed child must be checked the same way), medium to high for performance on slow machines until the gate has been passed on the operator's devices.

## Deferred — not currently justified

- **Scroll-under bars.** Real liquid glass lets content pass under a floating bar. Today's bars are layout siblings of the content, so nothing scrolls under them, and the prototype keeps that. Making content scroll under the header and the phone's bars changes every screen's insets; it has no evidence of need yet.
- **A full command palette of actions** (create a task, start a pipeline, jump to an agent by name). The palette here finds projects, System and settings. Actions and agents need a search index and ranking the audit did not ask for.
- **Inlining today's settings dialogs into Settings.** Step 2 opens them from rows. Merging telemetry, linked installations, relay and updates into panes is a later, separate change.
- **A morphing or refracting glass effect, tinted glass per project, animated washes.** Decoration with no function behind it.
- **Human-message navigation in a conversation** (the audit's F6) is a separate design with its own rejected drafts; this lane changes no conversation chrome.
- **Reordering projects by hand, monogram colours, a third tile letter** for variant 3: only if 3 is chosen.
- **No ADR.** Nothing here is decided, and every step above is reversible.

## Limits of this evidence

- The fixture's board is synthetic and smaller than the live one: one real project in today's rail, 16 controls in today's phone conversation menu where the audit's live sample had 55. Where the two differ, both numbers are given.
- Press counts for today's paths come from the audit's source reading and this run's menus; they exclude typing, scrolling and the final confirmation of a guarded action.
- Frames are Chromium at phone dimensions with touch emulation, and the keyboard walk is Chromium's. The operator's physical phone and its browser, VoiceOver and NVDA were not used; the walk checks focus order, the trap and names, and no screen reader's announcements.
- The prototype's menus are inert: a row's product action, its disabled states and its safety steps (stop a host, delete a project) are today's and must be carried over unchanged by the build.
- The contrast reading covers the text and functional marks the variants draw and, in 6 and 7, the glass layer's text, in the measured states. Body text on cards and in the feed is on today's solid surfaces and was not re-measured. A text another surface lies over in part (the product's notice over a column head) is listed as `overlaid` and not read, since its box holds the other surface's pixels. `prefers-reduced-transparency` cannot be emulated by the driver; its rule is read from the stylesheet.
- Blur performance is the two reviews' measurements, quoted above, on throttled Chromium with a software GPU. No physical device was measured, which is why glass carries a gate.
- The notice's row is drawn by pinning the product's notice to a measured band, because the prototype may not move a node the Viewer renders. A build renders it in the row; the geometry is the same, the mechanism is simpler.
- The prototype tells the phone's Overview from a project's board by the Overview's own search control. A build has the screen's scope at hand and passes it.
- Checks run for this lane: the kanban browser driver's `interface redesign` block (both tests, the frames, `measurements.json` and `interaction.json`), `interfaceRedesign.prototype.model.test.ts`, `interfaceRedesign.prototype.dom.test.tsx`, `layers.test.ts` for the z-index guard (its two offenders are in files this lane does not touch and are on main), eslint on the touched files, the type check, and the repository's publication hooks.
