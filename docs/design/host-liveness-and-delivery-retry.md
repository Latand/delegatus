# Host liveness, retirement and recipient-scoped delivery retries

On 2026-11-24 the operator requested one decision about the live host, the unfinished turn and retirement eligibility (#4283), followed by retries addressed to the recipient and the owner of each unfinished action (#4287). The 2026-11-20 architecture review, §4, lines 41–49 and §8, lines 83–91, is the originating technical requirement. The two issues share a design because a retry needs to know which owner can still act on which generation.

**Decision.** Extend the serving Viewer’s agent-state evaluator to answer for one exact host and turn. Let each effect owner verify that answer at the point of action. Schedule delivery work by obligation, with one controller timer for the earliest due obligation. A confirmed stop, a completed registration and a delivered message each require their own evidence.

## One decision for a host and its work

The serving `AgentStateService` owns acquisition and reconciliation. The pure model owns the meaning of the evidence, including the ordered automatic-retirement verdict. Give it the existing retirement policy inputs and preserve the first failed clause and its determined/undetermined distinction. Return the process state, native turn boundary, current writer, outstanding obligations with their respective owners, retirement eligibility and the evidence needed to revalidate the result. An eligible result names the exact subject and original observation. The policy owner for an operation still chooses the requested effect. This extends the earlier module contract and twelve-clause decision. `docs/design/agent-state-single-source.md:137–159,286–294,338–352`, `src/lib/agentState/model.ts:66–117`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredHostRetirement.ts:449–462,791–809`.

## Schedule the obligation that is due

**Identity and storage.** Use the canonical recipient plus obligation kind and stable source ID as the scheduling key. A message keeps its original operation/client identity and immutable payload through acknowledgement, including a journal retry leaf. Registration uses the exact session/generation and registration attempt. Bind the current owner, generation, claim/seat epochs where applicable, and source revision as conditions on that key. An ownership transfer replaces its scheduled instance. A fenced callback from the predecessor cannot complete the replacement. Store acceptance time, next eligible check and absolute settlement deadline separately. A later signal cannot postpone a deadline. Source rows retain payload, custody and disposition. The controller’s due index contains only keys, conditions and times and can be rebuilt. `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryController.ts:406–447,2201–2223`, `src/lib/runtime/startupRowRetries.ts:13–22,68–82`, `src/lib/runtime/sendSettlement.ts:973–991,1519–1535`.

1. **Publish after durable acceptance.** The producer supplies the recipient, obligation and due time when its reservation, outbox transition, registration failure or startup deferral commits. Host turn/attention changes and native queue changes release only dependent keys for that recipient. Echoes name individual operation IDs. Preserve only the distinct obligations actually owed. For example, registration success removes registration work and wakes a blocked dispatch. Acknowledgement success removes only the matching message obligation. Preserve recorded notice closure if a startup row is already resolved. `src/lib/runtime/structuredMessageDelivery.ts:2309–2312`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryController.ts:2094–2103,2133–2144,2205–2223`, `src/lib/runtime/startupRowRetries.ts:19–22,85–88`, `src/lib/runtime/startup.ts:3241–3267`.
2. **Run one due instance.** Keep the earliest actionable time for each key. Repeated hints coalesce. A readiness change can advance the next check, subject to owner backoff and admission fences. One timer per serving controller wakes at the minimum, disarms on empty, and re-arms after a bounded batch. Use due time then stable key for fair ordering. A key already in flight can owe one later check. Compare its version on completion so a concurrent change cannot be cleared. Serialize native effects through the existing recipient lane and admission order. Before a native effect, claim the original operation conditionally under its present custodian and run the #4283 effect check. An in-memory scheduler claim alone grants no dispatch authority. Let independent receipt reads or registrations finish separately even on the same host. A slow recipient cannot hold other due recipients past the pass budget. `src/lib/runtime/deliveryWake.ts:5–22`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryQueue.ts:978–999,1028–1055,1272–1290`, `src/lib/agent/registry.ts:12925–12968`.
3. **Keep the address through every caller.** Replace the unaddressed retry callback and separate inherited/row/deferred timers with scheduling of the specific remaining action. Carry the key through replay, failure, native reconciliation and watchdog replacement, as well as initial admission. Resolve the exact host only when execution needs it. With no owed delivery work, a normal delivery wake performs zero host visits. For account-wide credit changes select affected registrations under the account guard’s authority and keep its pre-dispatch refusal. A migration guard request still gets its bounded acknowledgement. Preserve startup adoption, pipeline deferral and independent resource maintenance as their own work. `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliverySignal.ts:19–60`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredControls.ts:490–511,621–653`, `src/lib/runtime/nativeQueueHttp.ts:60`, `src/lib/accounts/migration/conversationCommand.ts:302–343`, `src/lib/accounts/migration/controller.ts:127`, `src/lib/accounts/liveLimits.ts:70`, `src/lib/team/memberRevocation.ts:236`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryController.ts:1117–1175,1660–1686,2355–2383`, `src/lib/runtime/startup.ts:4478–4492,4594–4611`.
4. **Recover without a host walk.** On release acquisition or crash, fence the old executor and reconstruct due work from the runtime journal’s pending effects and retained terminal receipts, registry reservations/operation owners, and startup retry records. Read indexed pages with a stable cursor per source and source identity. Bind each row by its original recipient and action. A legacy row without retry fields gets its deadline from the original admission or existing startup policy. Unknown bindings stay owed to recovery with a reason. Never infer an empty queue from a failed page. Acknowledge a terminal receipt only after its outcome is durable. `src/runtime-host/journal.ts:2381–2417`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryController.ts:536–575,2355–2383,2521–2535`, `src/lib/runtime/startupRowRetries.ts:45–65`, `src/lib/runtime/sendSettlement.ts:973–991`.
5. **Close the missed-notification window.** Subscribe before taking the recovery cut. Drain committed changes after the cut, deduplicating by source ID and revision. Use a durable source watermark or pending-work marker for a commit whose hint was lost while the Viewer stayed alive. A bounded watchdog checks for an overdue known key or a gap in that marker. A gap schedules a journal page. Bound each page by count and elapsed time, yield, and resume from the last safely represented row. Pin in-flight keys. Limit the in-memory due window to the earliest page, refill by indexed deadline, and retain overflow at its durable source. A restart that loses the in-memory index must replay outstanding rows even if a previous scan finished. A stalled or replaced journal keeps the recovery cursor pending or restarts it against the new identity. `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryQueue.ts:1552–1628`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryController.ts:536–565,2626–2649`, `src/lib/runtime/deliveryProgress.ts:645–663`, `src/runtime-host/journal.ts:2381–2406`.

**Admission, dispatch and confirmation have different outcomes.** At each due check consult the actual holder. An undelivered, pristine queued operation may dispatch under the existing claim. If a send may already have started, query its original operation/current leaf and matching destination evidence. A late echo can settle it without another engine write. An expired unproved dispatch ends with unknown disposition and retains its duplicate-risk evidence. A proved pre-dispatch refusal follows the existing unsent recovery policy. Progress persistence has its own retry. Preserve the pre-effect flush where host recovery requires it. When that flush fails, the recovery action stays owed under the same key. Preserve the writer admission deadline from merged PR #4255 and measure contention separately from the migration-stop admission failure in issue #4212. `src/lib/runtime/sendSettlement.ts:494–556,1106–1144,1224–1235`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryController.ts:406–447,553–575`, `src/lib/runtime/deliveryProgress.ts:39–45,78–89,648–663`, `src/lib/runtime/structuredDeliveryQueue.ts:3730–3735`, `src/lib/agent/sqliteRegistryStore.readOnlyView.test.ts:365–389`, `src/lib/agent/registry.migrationStop.test.ts:340–373`.

**Seat wake accounting.** Resume only an existing designated seat by durable conversation ID. Recheck the epoch before dispatch and on confirmation. A still-held wake retains its original client identity. Withdraw a rotated wake from the actual holder. If dispatch already passed the withdrawal boundary, record “too late” and keep the successor’s work owed. Only a confirmed landing for the proper epoch advances the wake stamp and lifecycle cursor. The project’s configured wake interval, default 60 minutes, survives rotation. A disabled wake still permits the project check. `docs/adr/0001-seat-tick-wake-resumes-a-dead-seat-host.md:28–49,54–105,116–139`, `src/lib/monitor/seatTickController.ts:1125,1198,1279,2071`.

## Deferred — not currently justified

Keep startup census repair, cold-history retention and Git settlement under their existing owners. A shorter global polling interval would retain the fleet-dependent work. A second delivery journal would add another receipt owner to reconcile after a crash. The requested scope needs a rebuildable due index and keyed evidence reads. It does not require a new queue service, a fleet cleanup, a change to seat cadence or work on the other architecture-review items.
