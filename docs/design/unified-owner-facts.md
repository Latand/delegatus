# Read one conversation, retire one attempt root, judge one check

On 20 November 2026 the requester asked for one implementation of three changes. Opening the designated conversation must work independently of board construction. Ending work must schedule the temporary directories it created. A stage, a push and a batch merger must agree on the work required to verify a candidate and use the same verified result wherever their inputs and authority agree. The public requirements are #4280, #4281 and #4282. The design was first written against main at `50e53c0031` (20 November 2026). It was re-checked on 22 November 2026 against main at `7bc9b9de0`: every file, symbol, line and behaviour it cites was read again, the merged work in the Status section below is accounted for, and the two open pull requests that overlap the phases (#4371 and #4372) were read from their diffs. Line numbers describe `7bc9b9de0` and drift; the symbol is the stable handle.

## Decision

Record each fact where its owner can first establish it. Give consumers a keyed read and make the consumer of an irreversible effect check that the fact is still current. A deadline nominates a root for inspection. A passing test nominates a particular check for reuse. Neither fact grants permission to act on a changed subject.

| Module and owner | Interface at the seam | Implementation and adapters | Depth, leverage and locality |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Conversation identity, owned by the agent registry | Resolve an ID or former path to one currently readable entry | Indexed generations, aliases, path ownership and materialization, followed by one scanner metadata read. The desktop dock and the phone use the same HTTP adapter. | Panels can bind a conversation whose row has never appeared on their board. A change in generation resolution has one implementation. |
| Attempt retention, owned jointly by the root allocator for identity and the attempt/lane or standalone host for lifetime | Register and release a root, observe an owner transition, take due roots | The existing attempt SQLite inventory and leased retention helper. Pipeline and standalone registry adapters supply different terminal proofs. | A close or reopen reaches both scratch and config roots. One removal implementation still protects every producer. |
| Local verification, owned by the gate runner | Plan a subject, satisfy a check, read its verified outcome | The existing planner, bounded receipt store, owned runner and broker. Stage CLI, Git hook and batch runner are adapters. | Changing which test exercises publication or what invalidates its result has one place to change. |

**There is no shared storage or execution seam across these three modules.** Conversation identity survives indefinitely, scratch ownership ends after physical retirement, and check evidence expires or becomes inapplicable with its inputs. A common fact table, event bus or invalidation service would add a fourth lifetime. The common design is the ownership rule.

### Top recommendation

Land the conversation reader first. A cold open with the board queue blocked falsifies its contract in one user action, and the board reader remains available throughout the switch. Next land the lifetime writes before replacing selection so a missed event cannot strand a root. Migrate the gate consumers last, starting with the planner and ending at the batch publication boundary.

### Manager authority by identity

`authorizedManagerSeats` (`orchestrator/authority.ts:88–116`) takes `activeSeats()` and `revocations()` as sources. Its production callers supply every active seat across all projects and every durable revocation (`orchestratorWorkspacePrincipalsOrUnknown` in `orchestrator/seats.ts`, reading `readOrchestratorSeatStore(["slots", "revocations"])`). The rule has two fail-closed clauses that a project-slot read cannot reproduce. A conversation named by seats in two different projects gets no authority. A revocation at an epoch at or above the seat's epoch ends it, including a revocation written under a former conversation ID that now aliases the canonical one. A cold read that called the helper on the full sources would traverse every seat row and revocation row, so its cost would grow with unrelated seat history, and a read that looked only at the project slot would miss a duplicate claim in another project or a revocation under a former ID. Phase 1 adds an owner-backed keyed read to the seat store and keeps one implementation of the rule:

* **Index.** Additive partial expression indexes on `state_rows` for the collection `orchestrator_seats`, in the form of `state_rows_task_project` (`sqliteStateStore.ts`): one over `json_extract(value_json,'$.conversationId')` for the active `seat:` rows, one over the same path for the `revocation:` rows. They use `json_extract` only, so they compile on SQLite 3.39, and they build from existing rows with `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS` under the startup mutation fence, so no table, trigger or backfill is added. A reader that finds an index absent returns retryable unavailability and withholds any partial answer.
* **Read.** `seatClaimsByConversation(ids)` takes the canonical ID and every former ID that resolves to it, and returns the active seat rows that name any of them plus every revocation that names any of them, by index range scan at one collection revision. The former IDs come from the registry's existing alias lookup index (`registry_lookup_alias`, `sqliteRegistryStore.ts:396`), probed once per hop of the chain within the same synchronous `withConversationAliases` generation. The alias chain is bounded, and a cycle fails closed.
* **Decision.** The keyed rows are passed to `authorizedManagerSeats` as its `activeSeats` and `revocations` sources with the canonical ID as `callerId`. The function stays the single implementation of the clauses, and the registry facts (a generation exists, not superseded, project agreement) are checked as today. **Attempt clock.** For a root whose launch has `retainUntilClose = false`, the scratch deadline is the terminal attempt's authoritative completion time (a retired launch uses its `retiredAt`) plus 24 hours plus one millisecond, the existing `ATTEMPT_SCRATCH_EXPIRY_OFFSET_MS`. The completion time must be a finite instant not earlier than the root's creation time. Otherwise the root stays held and goes to the audit. The deadline nominates the root for inspection and grants no permission to remove it. It is withheld, and a recorded one revoked, while any of these hold at the current owner revision:

* the attempt is running, spawning, activated or has an unresolved termination;
* the attempt is the newest attempt of the stage the publication cursor points at. A comparison takeover can reactivate the cursor on an already passed attempt, so the cursor retains it until it advances again;
* the lane carries an explicit hold that names scratch: a requested activation close, unconfirmed hosts or a pending remote action. While such a hold applies the root follows the lane clock, and once it clears the attempt clock applies again;
* the lane is closed or completed and its finish is not yet established, which leaves the root held until `laneFinish` reports it.

A root with `retainUntilClose = true` follows the lane clock (`laneFinish`), because its owner asked to keep it until close. Live consumers are not a scheduling input. ## Deferred — not currently justified

**Attention indexes and compiler exports.** The bounded attention sources already support the designated seat through the shared overlays. A second persisted representation would add catch-up and failure modes to every writer. The board's batch implementation already serves its own consumers. A separate clean export for every types miss would discard a lane's warm compiler state on the next push. These cuts are reflected in phases 1 and 5.

**Engine split and behavioral selection.** The design considered moving 4,007 lines into launch, settlement and publication modules, keeping shared controller changes on full coverage. The review replayed every zero-context engine hunk on parent and child across the 42 first-parent commits from `git log --first-parent --since=2026-11-06T10:25Z --until=2026-11-20T10:25Z --format=%H 50e53c0031 -- src/lib/pipelines/engine.ts`. Two commits (`97d224fbfe`, `4fe10c6a11`) localized to launch and one (`2451da7daa`) to settlement. Four also changed imports or declarations absent at the base, and 35 touched the shared facade. The frozen inventory has 26 launch, 43 settlement, five publication and 16 transition files. Including required secondary owners gives selections of 90, 90 and 64 respectively. No commit localized to publication. **Zero of 42** would get a smaller set under this cut. The replay was not extended past that window: one later commit (#4196) also changes `engine.ts`, and the tracked `engine.*.test.ts` family still has 90 files. Counts describe selection and make no CPU claim. A future proposal would need to demonstrate a smaller safe selection on actual changes around patching, admission, port wiring or run-stage ticks before moving those declarations.

**Local forgery resistance.** A writer with control of the shared checkout and cache can fabricate local evidence or replace its hook. A checksum detects corruption and does not authenticate that writer. Protecting against such a writer would also require trusted publication entry points outside the checkout. The requirement permits local reuse when the implementations share trust. Separate verification identities, a privileged installation, protected storage and forgery fixtures would serve a different requirement and are deferred.

General-purpose event sourcing, a new conversation database, redesigning the board cache or search, deletion based on directory names or age, changing Docker retention policy, remote sharing of arbitrary test receipts, a replacement broker, and a repository-wide rewrite of the pipeline engine fall outside this request. Each chosen change has a concrete consumer and a measurable deletion.
