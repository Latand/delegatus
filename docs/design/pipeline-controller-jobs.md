**First selection after a restart.** Compare the subject, token and recorded owner with current state-write authority. Use the existing hot-state authority record and the same writer-readiness check that guards SQLite commits (`sqliteStateStore.ts:353–369`). When the durable active writer revision excludes the recorded owner, the authorized successor can supersede that claim immediately by keyed CAS, even if the old process lives. For same-revision owners also verify process identity in the same host, boot and PID namespace via `processIdentityStatus`. A missing process, different start identity or proven reboot establishes death. Bound the off-thread observation to 500 ms. Proven death also permits supersession on first selection, even with 170 s left. Where authority admits both or is inconclusive, a live or unverified foreign claim has its recorded deadline and monotonic cap. A traffic change or a lookup in another PID namespace alone proves neither loss of write authority nor death. A fenced successor cannot commit a permit. After logical supersession inspect the native writer, physical custody and receipt before any mutation. A surviving installer or Git child can still own an effect.

**Writes and revocation.** Factor the single-row asynchronous, deadline-aware `boundedPatchAsync` path behind `withDeliveryMutationAsync` (`store.ts:1148`) into a keyed pipeline checkpoint. In one synchronous transaction get the current ID, compare creation, state/control generation, subject fence, claim token, current step and expected domain revision, and put the new row once. Use the existing revision/lease checks at commit. Allow unrelated row changes by rereading and applying the transition to the current row. Treat a stale subject as superseded. No Git, host call, waiting promise or external notification inside the transaction. Checkpoint the next step and its deadline **before** issuing its effect. An unavailable checkpoint authorizes zero new effects. Publish a wake after commit. The row-change trigger already publishes the dirty generation if the process dies before the wake.

On completion persist an observed receipt or consume the result with the domain transition in one conditional write. When the apply lease refuses admission, retain the previous durable step and its expected result. On the next selection re-read both the lane and the native effect. If the previous checkpoint committed and only its reply was lost, acknowledge the recorded transition. If the effect completed and its result write never committed, validate the external receipt, then consume once. On unknown external completion reconcile the same key. Retry dispatch only after a definitive no-effect observation or where the adapter proves the operation idempotent. A rejected write inside a callback has ambiguous outcome until read back. Preserve closure, pause, restart, cancellation, delivery takeover, prior attempt retirement and changes to the expected branch/head. A late positive receipt can record historical custody under the existing domain rules, never revive a superseded lane.

**Clock and timer seam (phase 1).** `ControllerJobPorts.clock` supplies `wallMs()`, `monotonicMs()` and `arm(delayMs, wake): cancel`. The production adapter uses UTC epoch time, monotonic performance time and a cancellable timer. All job deadlines, retry eligibility, owner rechecks and scheduler wakes use this clock, including the scheduling-time argument passed into the store. Wire the same clock into the due-time wake adapter of the scoped controller and coalesce timer callbacks by claim generation. The deterministic adapter advances either clock and delivers ready callbacks in order, including a captured stale callback. In a fresh process initialize its wall epoch independently and reconstruct the monotonic interval once. The supervisor enforces time on the helper. Tests advance its clock only after a real effect or checkpoint reaches a barrier. Keep a separate real-clock harness watchdog for test cleanup.

**Two deadlines.** A pass returns on its current scheduling deadline. Its admitted job gets an independent operation lease driven by the recorded step deadline. At expiry revoke that permit, signal its owned work, record the interrupted step and reason, and enqueue reconciliation. A restart can derive expiry from the prior durable timestamp. If SQLite itself refuses the timeout write, the *already recorded* expired step remains readable. Stop effects immediately, schedule a bounded state retry, and checkpoint the reason against that same claim at the first successful admission. Each state attempt uses `min(remaining, 500 ms)` with asynchronous admission and yields to peers on refusal. The watchdog/fair cursor remains a crash fallback. Store outage cannot support a promise of a new durable write while the disk is unavailable.

**Capacity and cleanup.** Try capacity and physical custody without blocking. Keep waiters in their lane records. In phase 1 maintain a derived SQLite index of waiting physical resource, original enqueue order and lane ID in the same transaction as step entry/exit. Claim the head by indexed lookup under the keyed CAS. Wake one successor on release. Reconstruct missed wakes from due/fair selection. Use a stable enqueue sequence (row order breaks ties) across retries and clock changes. Release partial acquisitions before yielding. The common Git turn ends after verified checkout and provenance. Checkout custody continues through tool preparation. Each pool tracks one local handle per admitted executor. A draining executor with a live mutating child counts until close or verified death. A waiter retains zero local promises and zero execution permits. Existing execution and stop-read pools each keep their four-slot bounds. Reserve at most four additional in-flight inline native activation claims in phase 2. Advance the selection cursor even at full capacity.

**Physical custody (phase 1).** Before add, resolve the nearest existing checkout parent and append its missing path segments. Recheck physical identity after creation. If a symlink, case alias or missing identity cannot be resolved consistently, record a verification refusal before mutation. Use two disjoint sets of stable lock files under the installation's private state root, `controller-locks/repo/<stripe>` and `controller-locks/checkout/<stripe>`, each with 256 slots keyed by a hash of the canonical filesystem identity (including volume and platform case semantics). ## Risks and acceptance review

- **Persisting too much work under the lease.** One bounded patch may still serialize a large lane. Measure maximum-sized lane payload, writes per successful step, state admission latency and warm loop delay. Keep coarse durable transitions and diagnostic-only inner operations. If a measured extremum breaks the existing phase budget, move preparation/serialization outside the short revision-checked commit or narrow the existing store projection before rolling out additional adapters.
- **Losing work on successful writes with lost acknowledgments.** Fault both sides separately at every effect/checkpoint pair. Verify the actual ref, job generation, commit/launch/provider receipt and lane state from a second process. A retry that silently selects a new head or reservation fails acceptance.
- **Starvation under capacity, CPU contention or resource cleanup.** Keep absolute deadlines visible, exclude confirmed CPU queue time only where the native policy already does, preserve wake/cursor fairness, and cap retry metadata. Exercise a flood of dirty lanes, long-lived shared cache writer, skipped ticks, simultaneous expiry and restart. Publish latency to an unrelated first child alongside the failing lane's latency.
- **Unsafe compatibility or misleading UI.** Refuse ambiguous legacy evidence, show the actual waiting step with its timestamp, and test full and compact readbacks during state contention. Never display success for a verified checkout whose dependencies still lack a usable receipt.

The phase-1 functional verdict requires a red base, green recovery across a fresh process, adoption of the same checkout, one first child and a visible step and age. Report the issue’s separate one-minute desktop acceptance from the measured workload and subsequent deployment observation. Subsequent landings need their effect-specific proof, migration coverage and measured test selection. Recheck the issue's desktop condition after deployment. Scope here is the design; no implementation timing, rendered result or post-deployment acceptance is claimed.
