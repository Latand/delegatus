# Agent learning from operator prompts: retrospective audit

Status: observational research, 2026-11-05. Prepared for independent verification.
This report and its private evidence belong to the existing Delegatus benchmark.
Every discovered case is development/calibration material.

## Originating requirement

The operator's private voice request of 2026-11-05 asks for a historical audit
across projects: connect repeated requests and corrective follow-ups with earlier
requests, instructions or memory, actual agent actions and later outcomes.
The private evidence retains the supplied dictated wording and its uncertainty.
This public document paraphrases it because private conversation quotations
cannot be published.

The later clarification prioritizes cumulative autonomy and quality on
always-new tasks that share underlying principles. Reviewers and fixers are
part of the system being evaluated. The audit therefore distinguishes human
repair from ordinary review, independent later opportunities from continued
repair of one task, and successful delivery from a completed intermediate stage.
Prompt length, token count and the number of stored rules are not success measures.

The scope authorizes historical reads and these documents. It does not authorize
changes to other projects, memory, accounts, running work or the benchmark pilot.

## Failure mechanisms and useful controls

**A lesson can be wrong before delivery is considered.** C01 promoted a detector
dependency into a capture requirement. C02 stored invented product advice and
redundant native policy. Improving retrieval of such content could increase
interference. Content validity and task relevance need their own judgments.

**A recorded correction can fail to reach the intended store.** C01 includes a
successful file-memory write and a refused learned-rule rewrite. The accepted
report to the coordinator is another distinct event. Acknowledgment cannot
collapse these into one successful learning operation.

**Scope can grow beyond its original conditions.** C07 directly shows work
delayed under a broad policy and the operator's correction. The seat attributes
its origin to an earlier backup that needed downtime; that original permission
was not reopened. The supported finding is scope interference under changed
conditions. Its exact memory-selection mechanism remains unknown.

**A successful review system still needs a truthful endpoint.** C02's reviewers
caught content defects before replacement acceptance. C03's second review
confirmed three repairs. Those are useful outcomes of the complete system.
The remaining concern is approval whose stated scope omits the latest change.
The newly discovered disabled-state defect was already present; it was neither
a repair-induced regression nor an independent new-task recurrence.

**Human intervention can finish an otherwise completed artifact.** C05 separates
a recipe being ready from its requested delivery. C13 separates an architecture
stage being ready from approval of its human-facing vocabulary. The former is
a repeated delivery reminder; the latter also clarifies editorial requirements.
Neither proves failure after the entire reviewed workflow was declared done.

**Access and runtime conditions change the observed result.** C08 and C09 share
a role/model and adjacent dates, yet their text/document channels have different
capabilities. The first has a destination readback; the second has explicit
membership refusals. Pooling them as successes or memory failures would lose
the cause of the different endpoints.

Positive evidence is correspondingly narrow:

- C08 shows context refresh and an exact destination readback after a valid
  timing deferral.
- C08 and C09 both obey the explicit evening restriction in complete short
  attempts. Their later continuations remain part of their respective tasks.
- C10 reads an applicable skill and then uses direct image inspection in a
  different project/domain. The skill's historical creation metadata predates
  C01, so this cannot be credited to C01's later correction.
- C11 reaches the authorized merge endpoint without another corrective prompt
  in the reopened interval. It is a lower-difficulty, older-runtime control.

These observations support the usefulness of review, explicit scoped
instructions and a delivered skill. They do not isolate the benefit of an
initial architecture or memory investment. No matched later task establishes
a net quality or autonomy gain attributable to that investment. C07 supplies
evidence of policy interference; C02 supplies evidence of redundant content.
No customer-level effect size is available for either.

## Denominators and uncertainty

This sample was selected to reconstruct mechanisms, with successful, neutral,
changed-requirement, access-failure and provenance controls. It is unsuitable
for a population failure rate or a model ranking.

| Quantity | Observation | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Returned catalog rows/groups | 16,790 / 15 | Metadata coverage; one source-total row unresolved |
| Case dossiers | 13 | Several stages/reminders belong to the same episode |
| Selected authenticated operator records | 18 | Sixteen initial source turns/decisions plus two H records; not 18 independent tasks |
| Direct later constraint controls | 3 | Two timing controls and one visual-inspection control |
| Observed task/skill delivery in those controls | 3 of 3 | Two explicit briefs, one actual skill read |
| Historical learned-rule injection established in those controls | 0 of 3 | Unknown delivery; absence of evidence does not mean no injection |
| Independently confirmed destination arrival | 1 selected text task | A document send receipt and a refused document send have different endpoints |
| Causally identified memory or architecture effect | 0 | No randomized or adequately matched comparison |

C02's two repair rounds and C03's repair/review stages are not independent
learning opportunities. C08's night/day attempts are one task. C12's six copies
contribute zero recurrence opportunities. The three later controls all receive
brief or skill assistance; they provide no unaided-memory success estimate.

Across models, only role/runtime metadata actually present in bound attempts
is used. The theme episode uses a Sonnet-family builder and Opus-family judges;
the knowledge repair uses the same Codex model family for repair and review;
the two timing controls use the same Sonnet-family builder; the visual control
uses Codex. Several seats and legacy runs lack verified model or binary-version
metadata. Task difficulty, role, review budget, date, account access and channel
capability remain confounded. No pooled comparison is justified.

## Measurements for the same benchmark

The unit should be an independent new task with a shared underlying principle.
A complete run includes its ordinary reviewers and fixers. Record both the
system's final quality and how much human repair was needed.

| Measure | Numerator and denominator | Required qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Autonomous fulfillment | Independently accepted tasks reaching the requested delivery endpoint without corrective human intervention / all eligible new tasks | Ordinary review/fix stages remain inside the system; unresolved tasks stay visible |
| Quality at a fixed budget | Independent task-quality judgment and escaped-defect severity at the same total allowed budget | Include architecture, learner, curator, review and repair work; report failures and unknowns |
| Human assistance | Necessary clarification, new scope, repeated reminder, diagnosis, prompt rewrite, supplied skill and manual rescue per task | Keep these categories separate; a settled-answer reminder is corrective assistance |
| First-pass system fulfillment | Tasks satisfying the original agreed contract at their first complete system delivery / tasks with a complete endpoint judgment | Builder-stage pass rate is a diagnostic; incomplete or unobserved delivery is unknown |
| Recurrence | Independent comparable later tasks repeating the principle violation / independently adjudicated later opportunities | Freeze the valid preference; exclude same-task repair and copied imports |
| Corrective rework after done | Reopened repair episodes after stage, artifact, system or delivered-task completion, separately / observed completions at each level | Preserve who declared which endpoint and whether ordinary review was still pending |
| Review containment | Valid defects caught before delivery, escaped defects and late defects / defects independently observable in the evaluation window | More findings can reflect better review or harder tasks |
| Review precision | Independently judged, deduplicated true findings / all independently judged true and false findings: TP / (TP + FP) | Judge validity against the artifact and agreed requirement at the review checkpoint; retain unknown judgments separately |
| Defect recall | Unique independently confirmed checkpoint defects found by review / all independently confirmed defects at that checkpoint: TP / (TP + FN) | Establish the defect inventory independently of the reviewer's findings; report observation-window and inspection limits |
| Reviewer-induced rework | Unnecessary changes, repair effort and regressions caused by false findings / independently adjudicated review opportunities | Keep this cost separate from precision, recall and escaped defects |
| Transfer | New tasks satisfying a previously established principle across changed domains / adjudicated transfer opportunities | Label explicit prompt, skill, human and coordinator assistance |
| Investment benefit | Later accepted quality/autonomy versus matched controls, including the initial learning/architecture cost | Report cumulative sequence results and later-task results; no retrospective causal estimate here |
| Interference/staleness | Applicable-looking stale, conflicting or overbroad guidance that changes an action / opportunities with observable guidance and actions | Require the contemporary version and concrete action; retain unknowns |

For review precision and recall, an independent judge maps findings to unique
defects at a fixed artifact checkpoint and labels each finding true, false or
unknown. Repeated reports of one defect count once; overlapping claims about
that defect share one entry. Deduplicate false and unknown claims as well.
Unreported defects in the independent inventory are false negatives (FN).
Keep checkpoints separate so later edits cannot retroactively validate a finding.

Retain unknown finding and defect judgments with their counts and reasons.
Precision remains unknown while finding judgments are incomplete. Recall
remains unknown while finding judgments or the independent defect inventory
are incomplete. A judged-subset estimate must name its numerator, denominator,
excluded unknown count and coverage limit. Unknowns never count as correct,
false or absent by default. A zero denominator is undefined (N/A), with the
counts shown. No findings with confirmed defects gives undefined precision
and zero recall; no confirmed defects gives undefined recall.

For the same artifact with two independently confirmed defects, a review with
one true and 99 false findings has 1% precision. A review finding the same true
defect with zero false findings has 100% precision. Both have 50% recall and
the same containment if that defect is caught before delivery and the other
escapes. Containment alone cannot distinguish their false-finding burden.
These examples are calibration checks, with no new historical measurements.

A missing final record needs a row containing task identity, last known action,
assistance already spent, endpoint status and the reason observation ended.
Otherwise later measurements silently discard stalled work.

The delivery/application chain should record, for each task and engine start:

1. Original request and dated preference revisions, with author provenance.
2. Actual bound brief, role/model/runtime and applicable operating conditions.
3. Candidate memory IDs, versions, scope and content hashes; selection and exclusions.
4. Delivered payload hash and receipt, including native memory and explicitly read skills.
5. Concrete actions demonstrating application, interference or non-application.
6. Reviewer findings, fixer work, human assistance and delivery evidence.
7. A separate later-opportunity row, including opportunities that never finish.

Self-reported use of a rule is supplementary evidence. Rule storage, injection,
acknowledgment and behavioral application remain separate variables.

The settled benchmark controls remain appropriate: quality at equal total
budget, a strong single-agent control, a fixed-team control, and separate cold
and accumulating sequences followed by unseen tasks. Reviewers/fixers belong
inside those controls. This audit changes neither the protocol lane nor its
pilot, and executes no model cells.

For development, adapt C02/C07 into tasks where relevant native behavior or
operating conditions must be checked before adding guidance. Adapt C03 into a
final-verification coverage case. Pair C05 with C08/C09 to distinguish forgotten
delivery, successful delivery and a legitimate capability refusal. Use C10 as
an explicitly assisted transfer case. C13 can calibrate the difference between
a technically coherent data model and a usable human navigation brief.

Retrospective cases and repairs derived from them must remain development/
calibration data. A future holdout must use new tasks and independently sealed
checks. Its expected solutions, hidden checks and outcomes must stay outside
learner/curator memory. Difficulty and access strata should be balanced before
comparing cumulative sequences.

## Deferred — not currently justified

- A causal claim that memory, a curator or architecture investment improved quality.
- A universal failure rate, model ranking or Delegatus benchmark win.
- Counting stored rules, shorter prompts or fewer tokens as learning success.
- Replaying historical tasks as unseen evaluation.
- Product instrumentation, harness changes, new model runs, helper agents or
  changes to any project's code, state, rules or accounts.
- Applying the older board audit's repairs; their existing continuation retains ownership.

## Verification and handoff

The private package contains the case matrix, project/date coverage, query log,
primary-read references, scope manifest and a stratified verifier handoff.
It preserves minimal prompt excerpts and exact primary identifiers privately.
The declared repository output contains aliases and paraphrases only.

The existing independent verifier stage should reopen at least the knowledge
repair, final UI-coverage gap, changed form policy, successful text delivery,
blocked document delivery, skill-assisted transfer, legacy/import controls and
current human-interface correction. It must verify chronology, authorship,
delivery proofs, opportunity denominators and privacy before accepting findings.

Document/privacy checks and the exact saved output bytes are bound by a
versioned private final-state verification receipt. It records the source
revision, checked final commit and report SHA-256 after the last edit, including
EOF changes. The original receipt describes an earlier EOF state and remains
unchanged as historical evidence. A new versioned hash index covers the final
receipt and its evidence; the index excludes itself, and the receipt contains
no digest of itself. No build, broad suite, model trial or live state check is
required for this prose-only audit.
